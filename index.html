Zhang v. Chen

A lawyer in a BC courtroom cites cases that ChatGPT made up. What the court did about it.

2024 BCSC 285

Where the ruling went next

Two years on, 25 decisions have cited Zhang v. Chen on fake case law. 21 of them are outside BC.

Every decision in the A2AJ citation index that cites 2024 BCSC 285, by court or tribunal and date. The first to cite it was not a BC court but the Trademarks Opposition Board, six months after the ruling.

Cites it on fabricated or AI-generated authoritiesCites it for something else

Source: A2AJ Canadian Legal Data citation index, checked 26 Nov 2026: 26 decisions cite 2024 BCSC 285. We read the passage around each citation: 25 concern fabricated or AI-generated authorities; one (2025 BCSC 2134) cites the ruling's family-law costs analysis. A2AJ does not cover every court (Ontario Superior Court decisions are not in it), so these counts are minimums. Zhang v. Chen is a family case; this map shows only how later decisions cite it. Lawyers

Know your lawyer: who has actually argued this kind of case, built from public court decisions.

Prototype · sample data

    Sample profiles with made-up names and placeholder citations, to show the layout. Live profiles will be built from published Ontario and federal decisions.

    What a profile shows, and what it does not.

    Shows
    Decisions where the lawyer appears as counsel, by year, court and area of law, with links to each decision.
    Does not show
    Win rates or ratings. Outcomes depend on the facts and the client, so a score would mislead.
    Licence
    A profile is not proof of a licence. Check the Law Society of Ontario directory before you hire.
    Corrections
    Lawyers can ask us to fix or update anything on their profile.

    LTB

    Ontario's Landlord and Tenant Board, read from its published orders.

    Prototype · sample counts

    Two in three rent cases in our sample were filed by a company or public body.

    Of 50 eviction applications for unpaid rent decided on 31 July 2026, 33 were brought by a corporation, a REIT, a housing provider or a municipality.

    Source: LTB orders via A2AJ Canadian Legal Data (Open Government Licence – Ontario). One day, 50 orders: an illustration, not an estimate. Full counts come from all orders.

    How a rent case moves

    An eviction for unpaid rent has to clear five questions.

    Each order answers them in the same fixed sentences, which is what makes them countable. Every branch gets its count once the full dataset has been processed.

    Many cases end earlier, by consent after mediation. A standard order can still be voided by paying the full amount before the sheriff enforces it (s. 74(11), once per tenancy).

    What the orders say helps

    Members keep asking tenants for the same three things.

    The filers list

    Who brings the most eviction applications.

    A public list in the spirit of Ontario's Sunshine List: organisations that file the most applications at the Board, by type and outcome.

    Named
    Corporate landlords, REITs, housing providers and public bodies with 25 or more applications decided.
    Counted only
    Representatives: number of appearances by application type, no win rates.
    Never named
    Tenants and individual landlords, even though orders print their names.
    Wording
    "Applications decided", not "evictions": many orders let the tenant keep the home by paying.

    Mock Interviewer

    A three-step interview-prep method any AI can follow, and a one-file practice room with an interviewer who asks, listens and follows up.

    1. Research the roleWhat the firm does, who interviews, what they test for.
    2. Know yourselfTurn your résumé into stories that answer their questions.
    3. RehearseOne question at a time against a real clock, then a debrief that sets what you prepared beside what you said.

    Housekeeper

    Builds a hearing-ready evidence brief: title page, table of contents, numbered tabs and pages. Version 2.2 adds a checklist drawn from real LTB orders, a payment-plan builder, section 82 and 83 pages, and a search for similar orders.

    Method

    Every number has a source. We say when a figure is converted or estimated.

    Updated Nov 2026
    Pay data
    Job Bank (NOC 41101), NALP Canada 2025 salary survey, Precedent 2026, Cravath, The Globe and Mail.
    Recruiting
    Ultra Vires recruit surveys (employer-reported, so counts are minimums), Law Society of Ontario.
    Costs
    Law school tuition pages, Law Society of Ontario fee schedule, CMHC 2025 rental market report.
    LTB orders
    A2AJ Canadian Legal Data (York University), which republishes LTB orders from the Ontario Data Catalogue under the Open Government Licence – Ontario. Fields are read from order text by rule; the code is in the Housekeeper repository.
    Citations
    A2AJ's citation index, which lists the decisions in its collection that cite a given decision. Why each one cites it is read by hand from the passage around the citation.
    Conversions
    1 USD = 1.40 CAD (26 Oct 2026). Hourly to annual at 2,080 hours.

    About

    What JuSpecula is, and who makes it.

    JuSpecula

    JuSpecula joins two Latin words: jus, law, and specula, a lookout.

    It is a place to watch the law from a little higher up: what the legal market pays and hires, how cases are decided and cited, what legal technology is changing, and free tools that help people deal with it.

    Data
    Every number has a source, and we say when a figure is converted or estimated. See Method.
    Cases
    Decisions explained in plain language, with the citation and what the ruling actually held.
    Tools
    Free and open source, built for students, applicants and self-represented people.
    Not advice
    JuSpecula is general information about law and the legal profession, not legal advice.

    JuSpecula is made by Fukun Yang, a JD candidate at Osgoode Hall Law School who has worked across civil and common law systems.
